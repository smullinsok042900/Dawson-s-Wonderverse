# Dawson's Discovery World — Canonical Game Design Specification

**Audience:** Dawson, age five. **Genre:** open-ended, close-up side-view exploratory creative play. **Platform:** mobile browser first (iPhone, landscape), desktop browser secondary. **Budget:** $0 in additional software/services unless owner later explicitly approves spending. **Project boundary:** completely separate from *Dawson's Wonderverse*.

## 1. Vision
A beautiful, continuous, scrollable, vibrant play universe. Not multiple worlds or levels. The character remains large and expressive on screen as the camera follows her through one giant connected space. Physical play structures and creative installations interconnect organically. A five-year-old should be able to wander, poke, paint, drag, bounce, mix, build, ride, and cause amusing reactions without reading instructions or following a mission. Exploration is rewarding even if the child never completes a challenge.

## 2. Nonnegotiable constraints
- **ONE physically continuous world.** No separate themed worlds, no level-selection screen, no isometric map, no zoomed-out overhead perspective; no loading sequences between activities.
- **Four interwoven activity themes:** (a) Mega Playhouse, (b) Imagination Studio, (c) Wonder Lab, (d) Adventure Park. Do not make them stand-alone worlds, separate menu items or scene loads.
- **Excluded:** dinosaurs; dress-up; gardens; candy kitchen; underwater adventure; space station.
- **Control mode C:** on-screen movement and jumping + direct touch/tap/hold/drag/drawing with the environment. Primary controls work on iPhone without keyboard.
- **Characters:** entirely original, magical fantasy creatures rather than ordinary human avatars; brilliantly colored, different silhouettes, fully animated and truly part of gameplay. No fake “animation” consisting only of independently floating appendages or static still pictures drifting along the screen.
- **Feel:** warm, imaginative, whimsical, playful, visually rich, responsive and child-friendly. No death or pressure, no accounts, paywalls, advertisements, timers required to win, or unsafe chat/social features.

## 3. Play structure — blend rather than divide
Examples of contiguous mechanics and geography:
- A giant slide curls out of climbing structure and lands by a paintable wall.
- A trampoline lets Dawson reach art platforms where she can activate paint rollers and stickers.
- Mixing paint affects the color or behavior of bubbles from a nearby machine.
- A bubble stream carries the character toward a chain-reaction contraption with pushable balls, tubes and a magnet.
- An activated gear or paint-powered invention animates a ride in the nearby attraction area; the attraction has doors or tunnels looping back into the play structure.
- Hidden doors, high platforms and crawlways offer secret routes without changing level.
- Each 5–10 seconds of movement should reveal a distinctive interactive opportunity; this is a **design target**, not a claim of current implementation.

## 4. Starting original playable cast
Existing prototype characters to preserve and improve:

**Pip — rainbow explorer:** fluffy colorful whimsical creature (NOT a human). Large distinct ears or tufted silhouette; energetic bounce, reactive ears, expressive eyes, running gait, jumps with pose changes, land and tumble recovery. Bright candy-pastel palette with contrast.

**Zuzu — star spark:** a different silhouette and locomotion rhythm, tiny glowing/floating or star-themed creature; playful sparkle trail and bounce; clearly a standalone mascot, not Pip recolored. Motion should use one coherent rig with attached parts.

**Jelly — squishy explorer:** translucent or iridescent rounded blob-like magical creature; body squash/stretch under motion and landings, wobbly appendages attached to deforming body, unique expressive facial features. Looks physically squishy and playful.

Specific features above are art direction concepts, not a claim they exist fully in Prototype 01. Target at least idle + locomotion + jump + land + touch reaction for all three. Avoid accessories/dress-up systems.

## 5. World interactive systems
**Play:** real bounce and slide physics, movable balls, climbable structures, interactive buttons, swinging or moving platforms, launch pads, tunnels, toys you can actually manipulate.

**Create:** paint/draw directly onto world surfaces with finger drag and persistent state; physical painting implements, color mixers with observable output, shapes or stickers that interact with nearby mechanics.

**Experiment:** water or bubbles, magnets, gears, levers, tubes, moving contraptions, chain reactions and launchers. A switch should meaningfully alter a world object or system beyond particle effects.

**Attractions:** Ferris wheel, roller coaster, playful arcade mechanisms and safe rides that physically carry the character and can be started or controlled by touch.

**Interactions chain:** machine/paint/ride systems should connect causally — give kids the pleasure of discovering how their choices affect the connected world.

## 6. Player experience
- Landscape fullscreen responsive Canvas, correct iPhone safe-area handling.
- Two prominent left/right move buttons and a large jump button; separate easy-to-reach direct-interaction touch targets.
- Follow camera: close enough for character and environmental affordances to be visible, without clipping moving bodies or blocking pointer interactions.
- Tap/drag targets are visually legible and respond immediately; show cause-and-effect without requiring an instruction panel.
- Save locally at minimum: character chosen, paint creations, activated machines, discoveries, position and any customization meaningful to gameplay. Recover gracefully from corrupt/stale saved data.
- Pause/resume and loss of focus should never leave movement held down.
- Sound effects child-friendly and optional. Audio should only start after appropriate user interaction; no breaking autoplay requirement.
- Strive for smooth animation on typical iPhone Safari; scale resolution and cull offscreen objects if needed.

## 7. Visual language
Art direction: high-quality colorful premium storybook cartoon, layered near/mid/far scenery with intentional palette, colored light, high quality organic shapes/expressive creatures, playful motion and shadow contact; NOT purely tiny iconography, not photorealism, and not a collage of mismatched clip art. The concept art image in package is aspirational only. Avoid showing that still picture as evidence of working gameplay.

## 8. Delivery definition of done
- Provide playable actual game (web page/artifact) AND full editable source checkpoint (HTML/zip, preferably modular code) for each major iteration.
- Test start; character change; left/right continuous move on touch; repeated jump; tap activation; dragging balls; painting and reload; ride entry/exit; sound toggle; portrait warning or handling; any console errors.
- Show representative screenshot(s) from a real running build, not just generated illustration.
- List implemented changes separately from planned changes and untested claims.
- Every milestone must preserve an original rollback and document meaningful changes.

# Handoff audit: current Prototype 01 (existing source, not aspirational promises)

## Verified source file
`Dawsons_Discovery_World_Play.html` is a ~45KB self-contained HTML file using inline CSS, JavaScript and Canvas. It contains a title/intro screen, touch-oriented controls, close-up scrolling rendering, and a functioning animation loop. It has no required external JavaScript packages, backend, API key or source sprites. Source is the canonical checkpoint; do not assume concept images are game assets.

## Currently implemented in source (by code inspection)
- 5200-unit-long SINGLE connected side-view world; camera follows player and culls distant objects.
- Three selectable creature types: Pip, Zuzu, Jelly. Characters are custom-drawn in Canvas, with basic body-motion effects. UI buttons currently use emoji symbols, which can and should improve.
- Left/right touch-hold movement buttons, jump button and keyboard fallback. Basic gravity, collision with floor and some platforms, double-jump, trampoline bounce and tap-to-slide.
- Direct touch/drag of loose toy balls with simple gravity and bounces; a paintable wall with selectable colors and stored brush strokes.
- Touch-triggered color mixer, bubble producer, lab switch, magnet, Ferris wheel and roller coaster; wheel and coaster place/animate player along a path; sound toggling and simple sound effects.
- Discovery toast/counter and browser `localStorage` save/restore of selected character, player x position, painting, discoveries, and some machine states.
- Colorful Canvas vector-style background and UI, and basic mobile orientation prompts/safe-area CSS.

## Do NOT overclaim current state
- The visuals are a first prototype, NOT commercial-game polished art. Concept images are not embedded as gameplay sprites.
- Tap interactions for some machines are shallow and largely visual; paint/experiment systems don't yet drive an extensive multi-stage physics simulation.
- Terrain is mostly one horizontal world with some platforms, not a fully detailed vertically interconnected architecture.
- The three creature animations have not been validated on a real iPhone. Their current Canvas construction is not equivalent to professionally rigged sprite animation.
- Earlier assistant reported Chromium/mobile emulation smoke tests, but those are not evidence of testing physical iPhone Safari or a formal regression suite.
- Earlier ChatGPT HTML/ZIP download links could not be opened by the user on iPhone. A web-hosted prototype URL was subsequently created: `https://project32440.websitepublisher.ai/index.html`. Hosting status and behavior should be independently checked if available, and future updates should include a usable ordinary HTTPS link (not just a locally downloaded .html).
